# Putting AntiFake into practice
## A checklist for investigating any false or misleading claim
It is time to practise what we have learned. We can work with a news story, a video, a photograph, a social media post, a piece of data, a WhatsApp message or a claim suggested by an audience.
The aim is not to prove that information is false. It is to investigate it together and go as far as the evidence allows.
## PAUSE
☐ What exactly is the claim we want to check?
☐ Do we have the original post, news story, video, image or data?
☐ Can we avoid deciding whether it is true or false before investigating?
## LOOK
☐ Who publishes it or makes the claim?
☐ Who is it about?
☐ Does it concern one person, or does it generalise about an entire group?
☐ Does it use words or images that provoke fear, anger or rejection?
☐ Does it construct an “us” and a “them”?
☐ What story is it telling us? What might it be leaving out?
## CHECK
☐ Can we find the original source?
☐ Does the person, body or media outlet presented as the source exist?
☐ What is the date of the information?
☐ If there is data, can we find where it comes from?
☐ Do other independent, reliable sources confirm the information?
☐ If there is a photograph or video, does it really correspond to the place, time and situation described?
☐ Do we have enough context to understand the information?
## THINK
☐ What do we know now that we did not know at the beginning?
☐ Was important information missing?
☐ Is an individual case being turned into a claim about an entire group?
☐ Does the evidence really support the conclusion presented by the message?
☐ Has our opinion changed after checking the information?
## ACT
Now we can ask:
What can we assert on the basis of the evidence we have found?
☐ TRUE: the available sources allow us to confirm the claim.
☐ FALSE: the available evidence contradicts it.
☐ MISLEADING / MISSING CONTEXT: it contains real information, but presents it selectively or out of context.
☐ GENERALISATION: it uses a case or a piece of data to attribute characteristics to an entire group.
☐ WE CANNOT VERIFY IT: we do not have enough evidence to reach a conclusion.
## What do we do now?
☐ We do not share information we have checked and found to be false or misleading.
☐ If we share it to debunk it, we provide the source that allows it to be checked.
☐ If context is missing, we add it.
☐ If it affects a group, we also seek out that group's voices and perspectives.
☐ If we cannot verify it, we say so: “We do not have enough information to know.”
AntiFake does not give us the answer. It gives us a path for finding it.
